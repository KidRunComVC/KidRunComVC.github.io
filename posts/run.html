Run

KidRunComVC / 31 October 2026 / 2 minute read

Run,

because the miles happen whether or not the kids, the injuries, or the charts cooperate.

The kids? Not an issue anymore. They're grown: a runner, a skater, and a student / hard worker who gets 20,000 steps in most days. The grandkids aren't messing around either. The oldest plays football, runs 5Ks, is headed for 6'4", and is a little brainiac. The youngest cheers competitively and just spent three months teaching herself how to do a 10-second handstand, and she'll tell you all about it with the vocabulary of a spelling bee champion. Cooperation: check.

The injuries? Evergreen. A stress fracture from a state park trail run. Heart palpitations that graduated to occasional atrial flutter. Back spasms that can put me on my ass for weeks. I will run a marathon or die trying, and lately the odds favor the latter. My advice to new runners: don't skip strength training. Most do. Don't do what most do. Following the herd isn't fun. It's painful and expensive.

The charts? They never cooperate, and they don't have to. Charts do what charts do. The hard part is me: doing the work to find the sweet spot, then entering, exiting, and managing risk by my own rules. Whether I pull that off on any given trade is up for debate. The run afterward is not.

Running is just like trading. Motivation is the starting gun, and it's gone after the first step. Nobody wins on the days they feel like it. They win on the hundred days they didn't, when they laced up anyway or followed the trade plan anyway. The training plan gets you to mile 20, and the trading plan gets you through the drawdown. When it counts, you don't rise to the occasion. You fall back on your training, on the road and on the chart.
