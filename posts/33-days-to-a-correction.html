Market History

33 days from an all-time high to a correction

KidRunComVC / 21 November 2026 / 1 minute read

It took 33 days for the S&P 500 to go from an all-time high into a correction. Ryan Detrick notes that faster drops tend to improve the odds of avoiding a bear market: of the 10 fastest ever, only 3 became bears.

We count 11 (2007 ties at 33 days): 7 never became bears, 4 did.

Analogs, not a forecast.

In those 7, the median path was up 21.3% a year later.

HUANGGEDDON: The Silicon Tantrum

The S&P 500 peaked at 7,819 on Oct 6 and closed at 6,910 on Nov 20, down 10%+ in 33 trading days. The green line replays the median of the 7 no-bear cases from that close:

  • 3 months: +9.5% (7,566)
  • Back at the Oct 6 high: day 89 (about early April 2027)
  • 6 months: +17.7% (8,133)
  • 1 year: +21.3% (8,385)

Range of the 7 after 1 year: +5.0% (7,255, 2018) to +41.4% (9,770, 1928).

The 4 that became bears (faint red, 1 year later):

  • 2020: +27.9% (Covid bear, then fast recovery)
  • 2000: -12.2%
  • 1929: -35.8%
  • 2007: -39.5%

Chart idea inspired by Ryan Detrick table (@RyanDetrick), Nov 21, 2026. Prices: Schwab $SPX 1970-2026; pre-1970 Yahoo ^GSPC (non-primary).
