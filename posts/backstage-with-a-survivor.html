Backstage with a Survivor

A minute backstage with Rupert Boneham, and the first cool celeb outside of comedy I met on my travels.

KidRunComVC

I was working as tour manager for comedian Todd Yohn on the Bob & Tom All-Stars Comedy Tour. Backstage at one of the first shows, an outdoor set at an Indy 500 afterparty in Indianapolis, I got to meet Rupert Boneham.

Rupert was the Survivor fan favorite who won the million-dollar viewer vote after Survivor: All-Stars in 2004. He'd been mentoring troubled teens in Indiana since the 1990s, and he used a good part of those winnings to start Rupert's Kids. The nonprofit pairs at-risk young people with a mentor who works with them one-on-one, and it helps them with job training, life skills, and a stable place to live while they get back on their feet.

Backstage was packed and hectic, and Rupert was kind to everyone back there. He was genuine, funny, and friendly, and his smile never seemed to switch off. I mostly tried to stay out of the way and not look too starstruck.

Looking back, he reminded me of a part of my own life. I was a troubled teen myself, and I picked up some street smarts the hard way. Years later, I worked as a regional manager for group homes serving at-risk youth. I'd like to think I helped a little, but mostly I learned from those kids. They could always tell who really cared, because I used to be one of them.

So when I learned what Rupert had been doing with his life, it made sense to me. The TV fame just gave him a bigger platform for work he was already doing, and I'm grateful I got a few minutes in the same room with him.
