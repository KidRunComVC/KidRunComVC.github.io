Backstage with a Survivor

A minute backstage with Rupert Boneham, and the first cool celebs outside of comedy I met on my travels.

KidRunComVC

I was on the road as tour manager for comedian Todd Yohn on the Bob & Tom All-Stars Comedy Tour. Backstage at one of the first shows, I met Rupert Boneham, which I definitely didn't see coming. He was genuine, funny, and friendly, and somehow even cooler in person than I'd imagined.

He was the Survivor fan favorite who won the million-dollar viewer vote after Survivor: All-Stars in 2004. The beard showed up first. I just tried to stand still and look like I belonged in the picture.

He'd been mentoring troubled teens in Indiana since the 1990s, and in 2004 he used his Survivor winnings to start Rupert's Kids. The nonprofit pairs at-risk young people with a mentor who works with them one-on-one, and it helps them with job training, life skills, and a stable place to live while they get back on their feet. The TV fame just gave him a bigger platform for work he was already doing.

I was a troubled teen myself (yes, I stayed in trouble), and I came out of it with some hard-earned street smarts. Years later, those smarts helped me land a job as a regional manager for group homes serving at-risk youth, and I watched kids turn their lives around. I spoke their language, I knew which battles to pick, and I actually cared. Kids like that can spot a fake in a second. I know, because I was one of them.

Rupert brought all of that back. The show was an outdoor comedy set at an Indy 500 afterparty in Indianapolis, and backstage was packed and chaotic. He lit it up anyway. His smile never switched off, and it was real, whether he was talking to a headliner or the guy hauling cables. He was a ray of sunshine to everyone he met back there. So when I found out where his heart had been all along, it made perfect sense.
