Morning Show, Study Hall

Written by Joey, edited with help.

I'm not paid by or affiliated with StockMarketTV or anyone mentioned here, and I don't own a membership.

KidRunComVC

I started watching the StockMarketTV morning show around 2022, after following JC Parets on Twitter, now X. What I liked right away was how plain he talks. He points out opportunities, and in the same breath he reminds you that managing your risk is your job, not his. His co-hosts Steve Strazza and Spencer Israel talk markets the same way, and he's always ready to push back when JC gets a little too wild with a take. That back-and-forth is half the fun.

Now that I'm studying for the CMT, the Chartered Market Technician exam, I started watching the show a different way. They aren't making predictions with deadlines, and they'll tell you not to copy their trades. They even joke that they might change their mind before the show is over. So I stopped thinking of it as a show about calls and started thinking of it as a show about setups, which are exactly the things I'm learning about in my studies.

The show is basically a live version of my textbook. They compare sectors to the S&P 500, read bonds and the dollar as clues for stocks, and name chart patterns in real time. That's just a few examples, too. They cover a lot more CMT material in their daily conversations, but I'm keeping this one short. So now I treat each segment like a practice problem. I pause the clip, name the pattern, and write down where it would trigger, where I'd be wrong, and how long I'd give it. Then I check back about a month later to see what happened.

That last part taught me the most. Even the best-known chart patterns, by most counts, only reach their targets some of the time. Naming a pattern doesn't make it a sure thing. Knowing where you get out is what makes it a trade.

I've seen a lot of market shows, and this one is the most transparent I've found. They show their work and they tell you it's on you. To be fair, it's a business. They sell paid alerts and memberships, so watch it with that in mind. That's exactly how a study partner should act.
