Moving averages

A smoother for a noisy line. Late on purpose.

KidRunComVC / 10 October 2026 / 3 minute read

A moving average is what it says it is. Take the last handful of closing prices, add them, and divide by how many you took. Next session, drop the oldest close, add the new one, and do it again. The result is a line that travels with price and refuses to flinch at every wiggle.

average of the last n closes
= their sum, divided by n

The lag is the product

Day-to-day noise shrinks. So does anything sudden. The average cannot answer a price it has not swallowed yet, so it lags. People talk about that lag as if it were a loose screw. It is not. An average of the past is supposed to arrive after the past.

Short, long, and still backward

You will hear certain windows mentioned until they sound official: 20 sessions, 50, 200. A short window hugs price and still twitches. A long window is the slow opinion of many closes. Traders use them partly because other traders can see the same line. That is a social fact, not a mathematical blessing. This page is not handing you a number.

There is a cousin, often called an exponential average, that weighs recent prices more and old prices less. Same job, a shorter memory, still a look backward. If you keep one thing, keep the direction of the glance. These tools face the wrong way for prophecy. They face the right way for a summary.

A cross is a raised hand, second

When a shorter average crosses a longer one, the event has nicknames and a small industry of confidence. A cross means the recent window has moved above, or fallen behind, the longer window. It describes closes that already printed. The move that caused the cross happened first. The cross raises its hand second.

In a choppy stretch, price will step across the average just often enough to tire a confident person out. The line sits in the middle while the market changes its mind. An average is a poor oracle on a path with no direction, which is a lot of paths, on a lot of days.

Use one as context. It will show the slope of a run of closes without making you stare at every tick. It will not tell you what to do next. Anyone selling the lag as a head start has misunderstood the clock.

The lesson

A moving average is the last few closes, holding hands. They will tell you where they have been. They will be a little late even about that.
