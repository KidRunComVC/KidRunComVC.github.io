Despite All My Rage: Missing the Pumpkins and Lacing Back Up

KidRunComVC / 7 November 2026

I raised my daughters on the Smashing Pumpkins, much the same way the generation before me raised their families on The Beatles. I even assigned each of my girls a song. “With Every Light” belongs to my oldest, “Beautiful” to my middle, and “Luna” to my youngest. “Stand Inside Your Love” belongs to my wife, and so does “Come with Me,” from Billy Corgan’s other band, Zwan. “1979” is my granddaughter’s, and “Bullet with Butterfly Wings” is my grandson’s.

So when the Pumpkins came to town on Friday, October 16th, 2026, I had planned an important night out with my wife, my mother-in-law, and all three daughters. I wasn’t just looking forward to hearing the Pumpkins songs that mean so much to us. I wanted to stand beside the people they belonged to.

The day before, Thursday the 15th, I was washing my car and felt a tug in my lower right back. I’d felt it before. I recognized the warning. I knew I needed to lie down. Naturally, I finished washing the car.

Then I drove home and walked to the mailbox with my daughter, chatting about life. When I got back, I sat at my desk for a few minutes, got up, and my back seized up. I barely made it to the bedroom, and after a valiant effort, I crawled into bed and got flat with my legs propped up, thanks to my wife. Thank God I’m married to a nurse! An acute muscle spasm was the initial telehealth diagnosis, and the possibility of a pinched nerve came later when the pain moved to the left side and down to the sole of my foot.

Once I was down, I couldn’t get up. I missed the concert, and I’ll never forgive myself or the universe for that, lol. I spent the next four days in bed getting nursed by my wife, who took wonderful care of me. It was bad. So bad I had to use a urinal, and even that was almost impossible for the first 4 days.

I wasn’t the only one who missed The Smashing Pumpkins show. My middle daughter lives two hours away and was counting on me to pick her up. Because of my back and not knowing how things would play out, she didn’t get to make the show. She was sorely missed by all the other ladies, and I feel awful about it too.

While they were at the show, they all sent me pictures and video clips. They all said it was the best live Pumpkins show they’d seen.

My wife also loves “Cupid de Locke.”

It was my mother-in-law’s first time, and now she’s a huge new Pumpkins fan. In one picture, her hands are folded in front of her mouth in disbelief at how much she’s enjoying it live.

“Bullet with Butterfly Wings” is my grandson’s song, but that’s my youngest daughter dancing and rocking out to it live.

“1979” belongs to my granddaughter, my lil June bug. She can sing it and knows all the words.

Then there’s the T-shirt. My oldest had the idea to take a photo of the shirt they bought me, and Lindsay had the idea to get someone to take it with them holding the shirt up, so she could later have AI add me into the picture. They found a way to get me into the family picture. AI also found some muscles I hadn’t brought to the marriage. Then they added my middle daughter in too, since she missed the show, along with the pugs she loves. I still missed being there, but they’d made room for me anyway.

I was extremely sad to have missed it. Depressed, in fact. That’s hard to admit. From bed, my mind went straight from missing one concert to wondering whether I’d ever run my marathon. I’m getting older, and even though I’ve completed two half marathons in my training, I couldn’t see myself getting back there if the injury turned out to be long term.

Eventually I managed to get up without Lindsay’s help, but I had to hold on to countertops and furniture just to move a few feet. It was incredibly painful. After three weeks of pain and diligence, I walked my first comeback mile on Friday, November 6. It took 39 minutes. It was painful but doable. I was sore that night and assumed Saturday was going to suck. But when I woke up, I felt rested and saw sunshine through the window. I knew it was time to lace up. As luck would have it, I finished just over 4 miles, walking of course. No running just yet.

I still have a heart ablation ahead, once my doctor captures more palpitations, and a left foot that never healed right and still needs an MRI to find out what’s wrong.

I hit a wall training for the half marathon on the rooftop track at the YMCA in Charlotte, NC. My knees were giving out before my willpower or my lungs did. Lindsay had been telling me to work on my core for months. Apparently I needed a second opinion from a chatbot before listening to the nurse I married. As I recover, core and strength training will move front and center.

My oldest is a fifth-grade teacher, and she runs the GO FAR Run Club at her school. Her 5K is Saturday, November 14, and it’s something I always show up for. Watching my daughter become a runner brings me more joy than I can express. At my age, almost nothing gets my adrenaline pumping, but when my daughters, my wife, or my grandkids level up in life, I finally feel something again.

The marathon still matters. Right now, I’m hoping I can show up for my daughter’s 5K.
